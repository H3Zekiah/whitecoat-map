## One application, most of the state

Almost every medical school in Texas uses a single shared application called
TMDSAS — the Texas Medical and Dental Schools Application Service. You fill
it out once and select as many member schools as you want. <Source id="tmdsas-about" />

Fourteen medical programs take it: twelve MD schools and two DO schools. <Source id="tmdsas-schools" />

Outside Texas, MD schools use AMCAS and DO schools use AACOMAS. If you apply
both in and out of state, you will be filling out more than one system, on
different schedules.

<Callout variant="warning" title="One Texas school is not on it">
  The University of the Incarnate Word School of Osteopathic Medicine, in San
  Antonio, uses AACOMAS rather than TMDSAS. Students who assume the Texas
  application covers every Texas school miss it entirely.
</Callout>

## What it costs

The application fee is a flat **$235**, payable by credit card, no matter how
many schools you select. <Source id="tmdsas-about" />

<Callout variant="warning" title="There are no TMDSAS fee waivers">
  TMDSAS states plainly that it **does not grant application fee waivers**. You
  may see claims online about a TMDSAS hardship waiver program — that is not
  what the official page says. Budget for the full $235.
  <Source id="tmdsas-about" />
</Callout>

The flat fee is genuinely good news compared to the rest of the country,
where applying to a dozen schools costs many hundreds of dollars in per-school
fees. Applying broadly within Texas costs the same as applying to one school.

## The timeline

These are the dates for entry year 2027 — meaning students who would start
medical school in 2027, applying during 2026. <Source id="tmdsas-deadlines" />

| When        | What happens                                                     |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| May 1       | Application becomes available                                    |
| May 15      | You can begin submitting                                         |
| August 3    | Early Decision submission deadline                               |
| October 1   | **Regular deadline** — everything complete by 11:59 p.m. Central |
| October 1   | Early Decision results announced                                 |
| October 15  | Letters of evaluation due; schools begin making offers           |
| January 29  | Your school rankings are due, 5 p.m. Central                     |
| February 12 | **Match results announced**                                      |
| April 30    | Deadline to settle multiple offers                               |

<Callout variant="note" title="Earlier than you think">
  Applications open in May for a school year that starts the following summer.
  If you plan to start medical school right after college, you apply in the May
  after your junior year — meaning your MCAT, your letters, and your essays all
  need to be ready during your junior year, not senior year.
</Callout>

Applying early matters. Schools review applications as they arrive rather
than all at once, so a complete application in June is read in a less crowded
field than the same application in late September.

## The match — Texas is different here

Most of the country works on rolling acceptances: a school offers you a seat,
you accept or decline. Texas medical schools use a **match** instead, similar
to how residency placement works.

Here is the shape of it. Schools interview candidates through the fall and
begin making offers. In January, you rank the schools you would attend in
order of preference, and schools rank the applicants they want. In February,
an algorithm pairs applicants with schools based on those mutual rankings. <Source id="tmdsas-deadlines" />

What this means in practice:

- **Rank honestly.** Rank schools in the order you actually want to attend.
  The match is designed so that trying to game your ranking works against you.
- **A February match result is the real answer.** Offers made before the match
  are part of the process, not the end of it.
- **Early Decision is separate and binding.** If you apply Early Decision and
  are accepted, you are committed to that school and withdraw everywhere else.
  You find out on October 1, before the regular process concludes.

## What is in the application

The Texas application asks for its own essays, not the ones you would write
for AMCAS. Plan on writing separate material for Texas even if you are
applying elsewhere too. You will also enter your coursework, your activities
and experiences, and arrange letters of evaluation to arrive by the October 15
deadline. <Source id="tmdsas-deadlines" />

</Callout>
