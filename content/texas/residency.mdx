## Why residency matters more than almost anything

Texas law caps how many non-Texans its public medical schools can enroll:
no more than 10 percent of an entering class. That means at least 9 out of
every 10 seats are reserved for Texas residents. <Source id="tmdsas-residency" />

No other single fact moves your odds this much. Texas residents apply in a
smaller pool, compete for far more seats, and pay in-state tuition — among
the lowest in the country. If you live in Texas or can honestly become a
resident before you apply, this is worth understanding early.

When you apply through TMDSAS, the application service itself determines
your residency status using rules set by the State of Texas. The schools do
not decide this individually. <Source id="tmdsas-guide-residency" />

## The two ways to qualify

There are two separate paths. You only need one.

### Path 1 — you graduated from a Texas high school

You qualify if all three are true:

- You graduated from a Texas high school, or earned a GED in Texas.
- You lived in Texas for the 36 months right before you graduated.
- You have lived in Texas for the 12 months leading up to the application
  deadline.

<Source id="tmdsas-residency" />

This is the path most traditional Texas applicants qualify under, and it
works even if you went out of state for college — as long as you kept your
Texas ties and meet the 12-month requirement.

### Path 2 — you established domicile in Texas

If you did not go to high school in Texas, you can still qualify by making
Texas your permanent home. You must have lived in Texas for 12 consecutive
months before the application deadline, and for that same 12 months have
done at least one of the following:

- Worked in Texas (gainful employment).
- Owned residential property in Texas.
- Owned and operated a business in Texas.
- Been married for at least a year to someone who has established Texas
  domicile.

<Source id="tmdsas-residency" />

The legal definitions behind these rules come from the Texas Education
Code. <Source id="tec-54" />

<Callout variant="warning">
  Simply living in Texas as a student does not establish domicile. Renting an
  apartment while enrolled in college, on its own, does not count. This is the
  single most common misunderstanding about Texas residency.
</Callout>

## If you are planning ahead

<Callout variant="note">
  The 12-month clocks are measured against the application deadline — and you
  apply about a year before you would start medical school. If you are thinking
  about moving to Texas for a gap year to establish residency, count backward
  carefully: the move, the job, and the 12 months all need to be in place before
  the deadline of the cycle you apply in.
</Callout>

## The details have exceptions

The rules above cover most people, but the full residency rules include
special cases — military families, certain visa categories, dependents
whose parents moved, and more. If your situation is not clearly one of the
two paths above, do not guess.

<Callout variant="warning">
  Residency classification follows detailed state rules with exceptions this
  page does not cover. Read the official TMDSAS residency pages, and when in
  doubt, contact TMDSAS directly before you build your plan around an
  assumption.
</Callout>
