<Callout variant="warning" title="Read this part first">
  JAMP is for students in their **first or second year of college**. If you are
  a junior, you have missed it. This is the single most time-sensitive fact on
  this website, and it is the reason this page exists.
</Callout>

## What JAMP is

The Joint Admission Medical Program is a state program created by the Texas
Legislature for students who are economically disadvantaged. It is not a
scholarship you apply to alongside everything else. It is a structured path
into medical school, and students who complete it are guaranteed a seat at a
participating Texas medical school. <Source id="jamp-home" />

Fourteen Texas medical schools and sixty-eight Texas undergraduate
institutions take part. <Source id="jamp-schools" />

Most students who would qualify never hear about it. Their high school
counselor does not mention it, their family has not been through this, and by
the time anyone explains how medical school admissions work, the window has
closed. That is the specific harm this site was built to prevent.

## What you get

- **Guaranteed admission** to one of the participating Texas medical schools,
  provided you meet the program's requirements throughout.
- **Scholarships** during your undergraduate years and continuing into medical
  school.
- **Two paid summer internships** with clinical experience and shadowing —
  the exact experience that is hardest to find when you have no family
  connections in medicine.
- **MCAT preparation** and stipends for educational and interview expenses.
- **Mentorship** from physicians and medical students. <Source id="jamp-home" />

Read that list again with costs in mind. The summer internships alone solve a
problem that otherwise costs students thousands of dollars and a network they
do not have.

## Who is eligible

**You must be economically disadvantaged.** Specifically, you need to qualify
financially for a Pell Grant, or have a Student Aid Index between -1500 and
7000 as calculated from your FAFSA. <Source id="jamp-criteria" />

**You must be a Texas resident** for tuition purposes, and a U.S. citizen or
permanent resident. <Source id="jamp-criteria" />

**Grades.** For entry year 2027, a 3.4 overall GPA is required, along with a
3.25 science GPA. In earlier cycles the overall GPA was a recommended 3.25 —
the requirement went up, so check the current criteria rather than trusting
older advice you find online. <Source id="jamp-criteria" />

**MCAT.** You take the MCAT during your junior year, as directed by the JAMP
Council, and need a total score of at least 502 to be considered for
matriculation. <Source id="jamp-criteria" />

**Coursework.** You need 27 semester hours completed, and two semesters of
organic chemistry with labs before the fall following your first summer
internship. <Source id="jamp-criteria" />

## When to apply — the part people miss

There are two ways in, and both happen early.

**Regular admission.** Complete 27 undergraduate semester hours during your
first year of college, and apply **no later than your second year of
college**. <Source id="jamp-criteria" />

**Early admission.** If you completed 27 semester hours of dual credit during
your senior year of high school — with no more than 3 AP hours counting — you
apply during your first year of college. <Source id="jamp-criteria" />

<Callout variant="note" title="If you are still in high school">
  Dual credit courses at your local community college can qualify you for the
  early admission route. That is a decision you make in high school that affects
  a program you have probably never heard of. If medicine is even a maybe for
  you, this is worth knowing now.
</Callout>

## What to do next

1. **Check whether your college is one of the sixty-eight partner
   institutions.** The list is on the JAMP site. If you are still choosing
   where to go, this is a real factor.
2. **Find your campus JAMP faculty director.** Every participating school has
   one. They are the person who actually walks you through the application.
3. **Look at the deadline.** The entry year 2027 application closed on
   2026-10-02. Application windows open annually, so if you have missed this
   one and are still in your first or second year, find the next one.
   <Source id="jamp-home" />

## If you do not qualify

JAMP is narrow by design. Not qualifying says nothing about whether you can
become a doctor — most Texas physicians did not go through JAMP. It is one
door, and it happens to be a door that closes early, which is why it gets its
own page.
