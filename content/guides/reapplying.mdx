Not getting in the first time feels like a verdict. It is not one, and the
Texas data is unusually clear on this point.

## What the Texas numbers actually show

For entry year 2025, roughly **2,100 of about 9,500 Texas applicants were
reapplicants** — better than one in five. About **30 percent of them were
accepted**, compared with about 35 percent of all applicants.
<Source id="tmdsas-dashboard" />

In the two prior years, reapplicants were accepted at about 34 and 36
percent, against overall rates of about 37 percent — a difference of a few
points. <Source id="tmdsas-dashboard" />

So: reapplicants are a large, ordinary part of every Texas applicant pool,
and they succeed at close to the same rate as everyone else. Reapplying is a
normal path, not a mark against you.

<Callout variant="warning" title="What these numbers cannot tell you">
  These are pool-level rates. They describe groups, not people, and they
  cannot tell you what your own outcome will be. What they can honestly tell
  you is that reapplying is common and frequently works — which is different
  from, and more useful than, a prediction.
</Callout>

## What actually matters now

**Finding out what was actually weak.** This is the whole task. Reapplying
with the same application usually produces the same result. Common culprits,
roughly in order:

- MCAT score below the range of the schools you applied to
- Science GPA, particularly a downward trend
- Thin or brief clinical experience
- Applying late in the cycle
- A school list that did not match your record
- Essays that stated motivation rather than showing it

**Getting an outside read.** You are the worst judge of your own
application. A pre-health advisor, a physician who has served on an
admissions committee, or a blunt friend in medical school will see things you
cannot. Some schools will tell you directly why you were not accepted —
ask them.

## What to change, concretely

- **If the MCAT was it:** retake, but only after a genuinely different study
  plan. Repeating the same preparation tends to reproduce the same score.
- **If the GPA was it:** take upper-level science courses and do well. A
  post-baccalaureate or special master's program is a bigger commitment worth
  considering.
- **If experience was it:** get a sustained clinical role, not a scattering
  of short ones.
- **If timing was it:** this one is free. Apply in May.

## Common mistakes

- **Resubmitting the same application** with a new date on it.
- **Applying to more schools** as the entire strategy, when the record was
  the problem.
- **Not asking for feedback** because you are afraid of the answer.

<Callout variant="opinion" title="Judgment call">
  Give yourself an honest deadline and a real answer about resources. Some
  people should absolutely reapply, and the data supports that. Some should
  look hard at [other paths into medicine](/guides/paths) — not as a
  consolation prize, but because they may genuinely fit better. Nobody can
  make that call for you, and anyone selling you certainty about it is
  selling something.
</Callout>

## Texas specifics

- Because the Texas fee is flat, applying to more Texas schools costs no more
  than applying to one. Broaden your list.
- If your first attempt was late in the cycle, applying in **May** is the
  cheapest single improvement available to you.
