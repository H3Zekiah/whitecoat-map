Here is the fact that surprises nearly everyone: **you apply in the May after
your junior year.** If you want to start medical school the fall after you
graduate, the application opens before your senior year begins.
<Source id="tmdsas-deadlines" />

Work backward from that and this year becomes clear.

## What actually matters now

**The MCAT.** It needs to be taken early enough that your score is available
when you apply. That usually means testing in the spring of junior year, or
in January of that year if you want a retake option.

**Letters of evaluation.** Ask people who have actually seen you work: a
science professor who knows your name, a research or clinical supervisor, an
employer. Give them months, not weeks, and give them your materials so they
can write something specific.

**Your essays.** The Texas application has its own essays, separate from the
national application. Start them before the summer you apply, not during it.

**Your prerequisites.** They need to be done or nearly done, with good grades.

## What to do this semester

- Pick your MCAT date and build the study schedule backward from it.
- Ask three to five people for letters, in person if you can.
- Draft your personal statement badly, on purpose, then rewrite it four
  times. First drafts are supposed to be bad.
- Get your activity list written down while you still remember dates and
  hours.

## What can safely wait

Your final school list. You can decide where to apply after you have a score
in hand — and applying broadly in Texas costs the same as applying narrowly,
because the Texas fee is flat.

## Common mistakes

- **Taking the MCAT before you are ready** because a deadline is looming. A
  delayed application with a good score usually beats an on-time application
  with a bad one — though "usually" is doing real work in that sentence, so
  think about your specific situation.
- **Asking for letters late.** A rushed letter reads as a rushed letter.
- **Writing the essay you think they want.** Admissions readers see thousands
  of these. The generic ones blur together.

<Callout variant="opinion" title="Judgment call">
  If your application will be materially stronger in a year — a retake, a
  finished research project, a real clinical job — take the year. A gap year
  is normal and carries no penalty. Applying a year early with a weak
  application, then reapplying, costs more time than waiting once.
</Callout>

## Texas specifics

- The Texas application opens **May 1** and you can submit from **May 15**;
  the regular deadline is **October 1**, with letters due **October 15**.
  <Source id="tmdsas-deadlines" />
- Schools read applications as they arrive, so submitting in May or June puts
  you in a less crowded field than September.
- Texas uses a **match** rather than rolling acceptances. Understand it
  before you rank: [how the Texas application works](/texas/tmdsas).
