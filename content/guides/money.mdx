Nobody tells you the price tag up front. Here it is, so you can plan instead
of being surprised.

## The unavoidable costs

**The MCAT.** Registration runs a few hundred dollars, and it goes up the
later you register. If you qualify for fee assistance the cost drops to
**$145**. <Source id="aamc-fap" />

**The Texas application.** A flat **$235**, regardless of how many Texas
schools you pick. <Source id="tmdsas-about" />

This flat fee is a genuine advantage of applying in Texas. Elsewhere you pay
per school, and a normal application list runs into many hundreds of dollars
before anyone has interviewed you.

<Callout variant="warning" title="TMDSAS does not waive its fee">
  TMDSAS states that it **does not grant application fee waivers**. Some
  websites claim a TMDSAS hardship waiver exists. The official page says
  otherwise, so plan on paying the $235. <Source id="tmdsas-about" />
</Callout>

**Interview travel.** This is the cost people forget and it can dwarf the
fees. Several interviews across Texas means gas or flights, a night in a
hotel, and time off work. Ask every school whether they offer virtual
interviews or travel assistance — many do, and they do not always advertise it.

## Help that actually exists

**AAMC Fee Assistance Program.** If your household income is at or below 400
percent of the federal poverty level, you may qualify. It reduces the MCAT
fee to $145, waives AMCAS fees for one application to up to 20 schools, and
includes a free two-year subscription to the MSAR school database — which
otherwise costs money and is the standard tool for researching schools. <Source id="aamc-fap" />

<Callout
  variant="note"
  title="Apply for fee assistance before you register for anything"
>
  The award applies to things you buy afterward, not retroactively to what you
  already paid. Fee assistance is the first form to fill out, not the last.
</Callout>

**JAMP.** If you are a first- or second-year college student who is
economically disadvantaged, JAMP provides scholarships, MCAT preparation, and
stipends for educational and interview expenses. It addresses nearly every
cost on this page at once. <Source id="jamp-home" /> See [the JAMP
page](/texas/jamp) — the deadline comes far earlier than people expect.

## What you can skip

Commercial MCAT courses cost thousands of dollars. They are a convenience,
not a requirement. Plenty of people score well using the AAMC's own practice
material — which is the closest thing to the real exam — along with free and
low-cost question banks and a disciplined schedule. If the choice is a prep
course or rent, pay rent.

Admissions consultants are likewise optional. Your school's pre-health
advisor, if you have one, is free. So is this site.

## The bigger number nobody mentions

Applying is the small cost. Medical school tuition is the large one, and
Texas public medical schools are among the least expensive in the country —
which is a large part of why Texas residency matters so much. See [Texas
residency](/texas/residency) for how that works.

<Callout variant="warning" title="Check current prices yourself">
  Fees change every cycle. These figures were checked against official AAMC and
  TMDSAS pages on 2026-08-01. Confirm current amounts before budgeting.
</Callout>
