A gap year is one or more years between finishing college and starting
medical school. It is common enough that "traditional" applicants — straight
through, no break — are no longer the only normal.

## The numbers, since nobody shows you these

Among Texas applicants for entry year 2025, about **2,300 applied as
non-traditional applicants**, and roughly **26 percent were accepted**,
compared with about 35 percent of all applicants. <Source id="tmdsas-dashboard" />

Read that carefully, because it is easy to read wrong. It does **not** mean a
gap year lowers your odds by nine points. Non-traditional applicants differ
from traditional ones in many ways at once — career changers, people with
family obligations, people repairing an academic record. The number tells you
this group is large and that hundreds of them get in every year. It does not
tell you what a gap year would do for you.

## What actually matters now

**Doing something you can explain.** The question is never "did you take
time off." It is "what did you do with it, and what did you learn." A year
working as a scribe, an EMT, a research assistant, or supporting your family
are all answerable. A year with no account of itself is harder.

**Fixing the actual weakness.** If your MCAT was the problem, retake it. If
your science GPA was the problem, take upper-level sciences and do well. A
year of unrelated impressive activity does not fix a specific gap.

**Money.** A gap year is often the first time you earn. Clinical jobs that
pay — scribing, EMT, CNA, medical assistant — do double duty.

## What can safely wait

Nothing much, honestly. A gap year is short. Decide early what it is for and
spend it on that.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating it as a break.** The application still needs building, and the
  year is visible on your application.
- **Applying during the gap year without fixing anything.** If you are
  reapplying, see [reapplying](/guides/reapplying).
- **Assuming you must do research.** Only do research if you want to; a
  clinical job usually teaches you more about whether you want this career.

<Callout variant="opinion" title="Judgment call">
  For students who worked through college or had no advising, a gap year is
  often the single highest-return decision available. It buys the time to
  take the MCAT properly, get real clinical hours, and apply early rather
  than scrambling. The people who most need one are frequently the ones who
  feel they cannot afford to take one — which is worth examining honestly
  rather than assuming.
</Callout>

## Texas specifics

- Watch your **Texas residency** if you move for work. The rules turn on
  domicile, not merely living somewhere. See
  [Texas residency](/texas/residency).
- Remember that the Texas application opens in **May** — so a gap year means
  applying the May before the year you would start, not during it.
